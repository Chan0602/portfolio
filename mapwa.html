How I Think

01 · Mapping student questions to gaps in the application flow

80+ post-round emails · 20+ asking about status or next steps

Mapping the process made the real issue clear: the steps worked, but the transitions between them didn't.

Students could complete the required steps, but the system rarely confirmed what had happened, where they were in the process, or what would happen next. Instead of rebuilding the workflow, I focused on closing those communication gaps.

02 · Making important information easier to prioritize

The first dashboard showed the system. The second helped admins act on it.

V1 · Information Display

Everything had a place. Nothing had priority.

Visible↓?↓Actionable
V2 · Actionable Dashboard
01 Surface exceptions 02 Prioritize urgency 03 Connect insight to action

I defined decision support as the dashboard’s core purpose, then reorganized the hierarchy around exceptions, urgency, and next actions, turning it from a passive report into an operating tool for admins.

03 · Expanding the system to support real-world edge cases

Many of the hardest cases appeared outside the expected matching flow.

I expanded the design beyond the happy path, defining how the system should guide students through the standard flow while making exceptions visible and actionable for administrators.

Reflection

What this changed about how I design.

01

Clarity before simplicity

Simplifying a workflow does not always mean removing steps. Sometimes the better solution is to make progress, status, and next actions easier to understand.

02

Design the system, not the screen

Student confusion and admin workload were two sides of the same problem. Designing across both experiences helped me see how one interface decision could reduce friction elsewhere in the system.

03

Edge cases are part of the product

A workflow is not finished when only the happy path works. Self sourced placements, unmatched students, and reallocation showed me that exceptions need to be designed as deliberately as the main flow.
